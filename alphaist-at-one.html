Alphaist at One: The Dawn of a Technological Revolution, and the Founders We Believe In

At the end of 2024, pessimism about startups was widespread, capital was scarce, and fundraising was difficult. I chose that moment to start Alphaist Partners because I could see the future approaching. AI and robotics were setting off a profound paradigm shift, and Chinese founders, in China and around the world, were at the forefront: ambitious, broad in their outlook, and fast to iterate. Just over a year later, what once took courage to say has become consensus.

There was a deeper reason, too. In a capital market where greed and fear play out every day, too many people and too much money chase the latest IPO themes and market consensus. The exceptional founders and long-term changes that truly move society forward are often overlooked, even ridiculed, in their early days.

We saw a group of founders the mainstream regarded as outsiders. They think from first principles, have seemingly unreasonable ambitions, and insist on trying when others tell them it cannot be done. They take the long view and believe that "too much entrepreneurship is driven by expediency and dubious reasoning; only a higher ideal can take you further." They are what Frank Wang, Wenfeng Liang, and even Elon Musk were at the start.

I call them Alpha Founders.

Alphaist exists for these people. Supporting them is the work we have chosen.

As we mark our first year of operations, I am writing to the founders who hold to their convictions and are brave enough to begin amid the confusion; to the LPs and partners who have placed their trust in us; and to those who are curious about Alphaist and may want to join us.

Why we started

I did not start Alphaist simply to be a shrewd investor and make as much money as possible. We do not believe in following the crowd and playing the odds, or in passing investments from one buyer to the next at ever-higher valuations. Our mission is to empower Alpha Founders from the earliest stages, helping them move society forward.

What is an Alpha Founder? These are not simply businesspeople skilled at calculating ROI or opportunists looking for a profitable trade. They are unusually focused, resilient people who often seem out of place and are misunderstood early on. They have:

  • Unreasonable ambition. They want to create a new paradigm or category, rather than make a quick fortune.
  • An obsessive commitment to the product. They work through every detail, as DJI did with Osmo Pocket.
  • An extraordinary capacity to learn from first principles. They develop so quickly that someone you met only days ago can already deserve a fresh assessment.

There is often no precedent for what they are attempting, and no clear business model yet. Investors focused on short-term returns and ROI tend to overlook them. Yet it was founders like these who built Tesla, SpaceX, DJI, and DeepSeek.

We believe founders should make the most of their years by pursuing ambitions large enough to matter. Money and success are not the only things that give the work meaning. To attempt something great is already to help create the future. Alphaist aims to be their earliest investor, to stay with them the longest, and to make the greatest difference. This is how we work. It means that founders and investors choose each other, that each depends on the other, and that the relationship is built on real trust.

Our first year

For the first time, we are sharing our progress over the past year. It shows that Alphaist is no longer a solo VC, and that it is more than a sector-focused early-stage fund.

1. Investing with conviction, before consensus

As of January 2026, we had made ten new investments in our first year of operations. They reflect a clear approach.

A range of stages, with substantial commitments. Individual investments ranged from several hundred thousand dollars at seed to $10 million at Series A. Most were between $3 million and $5 million. We do not aim to cover a market by making as many investments as possible, and we do not invest only in first rounds. In four companies, our first investment was made at a valuation between $100 million and $200 million.

Fast decisions and a short process. We may follow a founder for a long time and research a field for months or years. When it is time to invest, we move quickly. We avoid cumbersome processes and founder-unfriendly terms, and respect founders' time and choices. In our fastest deal, just one week elapsed between issuing a term sheet and wiring $5 million.

Independent judgment and concentrated investment. We trust our own judgment and do not want to be passive followers. In 90% of our investments, Alphaist was either the sole lead investor or one of just two co-leads. The market has since provided support for those convictions: 70% of our portfolio companies had completed or signed a new financing round within a year of our investment, and we had already made additional investments in 40% of the portfolio.

2. AI and robotics

We focus on Chinese founders in China and around the world. Alongside our experience in robotics and hardware, we are actively investing in new opportunities in AI and embodied intelligence.

A global portfolio. AI companies account for 40% of our investments by number, primarily outside China, across AI-native models and applications. Hardware companies account for 60%, primarily in China, spanning embodied intelligence, consumer robotics, and core components.

New forms of interaction and content creation. Fish Audio is opening up emotionally expressive interactions and changing voice content with AI. ACE Studio uses AI to give people greater freedom to create music. Robotic companions such as LOOI are changing how people relate to machines. Yaogan Innovation is developing entirely new ways to interact in three-dimensional space.

The arrival of agentic AI. As AI improves, it can carry out tasks with greater autonomy. Future products will increasingly be built for agents as well as people. Existing workflows will be overturned, opening large markets for new companies. One-person companies and ten-person unicorns are close to becoming real forms of entrepreneurship. We are looking for founders who will define this field, whether through vertical agents or agent infrastructure.

Embodied intelligence and robotics. AI is moving from the digital world into the physical one. General capabilities in perception, reasoning, and action will greatly expand what robots and smart hardware can do. We see considerable strength in China's efficient supply chains and deep pool of engineering talent. What excites us even more is the courage and judgment of young founders. They are not content to follow others. They want to build globally leading products and even define new categories on a global scale.

3. Young founders with fresh ideas, still waiting to be discovered

What has excited me most this year is the founders themselves. On average, they were 28 when they started their companies. Sixty percent began either before graduating from university or immediately afterward.

They bring fresh minds, with no inherited assumptions to defend. They are willing to reason from first principles, even when the result looks a little strange or crazy.

Mainstream VCs may see inexperience and risk. We see hidden gems. We are willing to back that apparent madness, because, as the line goes, the people who are crazy enough to think they can change the world are the ones who do.

Since entering venture capital in 2017, I have repeatedly seen my confidence in young founders rewarded. Three companies I backed while at 5Y Capital are expected to go public in 2026. When I first invested, their founders were in their twenties and fresh out of university. Most had neither revenue nor a finished product. Few believed they could become global leaders in their fields. That is why Alphaist exists: to stand beside these young people while they are still alone, before the rest of the market rushes in.

4. Fundraising: Trust is our greatest asset

Our founding purpose and experience in AI and robotics earned Alphaist more trust at the outset than we had expected. We attracted LPs who share our outlook: leading institutional investors and family offices in Europe and North America, several founders of technology giants, and many respected figures in venture capital.

In a fundraising winter, we exceeded the target for our debut fund in six months. With a twelve-year, dollar-denominated fund, we have given ourselves time because great companies take time to build. We will continue to keep sufficient capital available to support Alpha Founders throughout the journey from inception to scale.

Building the firm

Alongside an active year of investing, we have devoted substantial effort to building our organization and the community around it.

From one investor to a team. From one person at the end of 2024, the team now includes five full-time members and three expert advisers, covering AI, robotics, finance, operations, and content. We are now a small, focused team capable of helping founders and making a difference to their companies. We are continuing to grow in 2026 and welcome people with hands-on product and technical experience.

Beyond capital. Investment is the most basic thing we offer. We launched The Alphaist, a podcast built around in-depth conversations, to share what we believe and bring together people who think similarly. Our first six episodes covered mobile robots, AI voice, dexterous hands, and AI music. Each offered substantial detail and insight, and the response from founders has been encouraging.

We have also fitted out a new office in Nanshan, Shenzhen. We want it to be a place where founders can exchange ideas and share technical knowledge as well as a place to work. Our hope is that it becomes a home for Alpha Founders, where an idea that sounds crazy elsewhere can find people who understand it.

What comes next

At our first anniversary, we can see the changes that AI and robotics are bringing, and the possibilities they open for a new generation of founders.

The market has no shortage of early-stage funds, or funds that make money. What it needs is a fund built for founders: one that understands technology and products, commits substantial capital before consensus forms, and stands by Alpha Founders through their darkest moments.

We believe these founders can create lasting value for society and deliver strong returns to the investors who believe in them. Financial returns are not the only purpose. We see them as the natural consequence of helping build something great.

I want to close with a wish for the Alpha Founders who are always on our minds, and for everyone building alongside us:

As AI and robotics approach a singularity, may you lead the way, reshape the foundations of civilization, and create lasting value. Alphaist will always be your steadfast ally.

Peter
